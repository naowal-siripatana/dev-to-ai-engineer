Teach-back: RAG versus fine-tuning

30 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to explain in 5 sentences when RAG fits and when fine-tuning fits, with evidence from your own project.

1 · Warm-up: write down what you know 5 min

Close your notes. A colleague says: “Why not train the model on our manuals? Then it just knows them, no database needed.” Write what you would answer in two lines. Then write one thing that is good about the colleague’s idea.

Do not look anything up. Your guess is the most useful line you will write today.

2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: explain RAG versus fine-tuning in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note.

  • Who you are talking to: the colleague above.
  • Use these words: weights, retrieve, cite, update, permission.
  • Rules: no notes, give one number or example from your own project, and be fair about what fine-tuning is good for.

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

1. Fine-tuning ... : it trains the model on my examples, so the knowledge ends up in ... .
2. RAG ... : the model stays the same, and at question time I ... .
3. For knowledge that changes, RAG is better because ... (for example ... took ... ms).
4. RAG can cite and filter, because ... ; fine-tuning cannot, because ... .
5. Fine-tuning is the right tool when ..., not for ... .

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the six key points and tick each one that your explanation said clearly. Be strict.

  1. Where the knowledge lives: in RAG, in your documents, found at question time. In fine-tuning, in the model’s weights.
  2. Updates: with RAG you change the page and re-embed it. With fine-tuning you must train again.
  3. Sources: RAG can cite, because the chunk id travels with the text. A fine-tuned model cannot say where a fact came from.
  4. Permissions: RAG can filter the query per user. Weights cannot be filtered per user.
  5. What fine-tuning is good for: style, format, tone and special behaviour, not facts that change. They can be combined.
  6. Be honest about limits: RAG can retrieve the wrong page, and you have to measure it. You did not try fine-tuning, so do not invent its numbers.
A model answer in 5 sentences

Fine-tuning trains the model on my examples, so the knowledge ends up inside its weights, while with RAG the model stays the same and I find the right pages at question time. For facts that change, RAG is better: when I changed one page it took 13 milliseconds to re-embed it and the next answer used it, with no training. RAG can also cite the page and filter by role, which I checked on 18 questions as a customer with zero staff-only pages seen, and a fine-tuned model can do neither, because the facts are mixed into the weights. Fine-tuning is the right tool for style, format or a special behaviour, not for a list of facts that change, and the two can be combined. I did not try fine-tuning, so I do not claim its cost, and RAG has its own risk, bad retrieval, which I measure with a golden set.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

4 · Look at the evidence 5 min

Do not trust the argument. Here is what your own project showed (real runs; the writer was a stand-in, so the wording is not a real model’s):

UPDATE    before: The monitor supports 2.4 GHz networks only [faq-wifi#0].
          I changed ONE page: re-embedded and stored 1 chunk in 13 ms (no training, no new model).
          after:  The monitor supports 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks [faq-wifi#0].
SOURCES   every answer carries [doc_id#chunk]: the reader can open the page.
PERMISSION  18 golden questions asked as a customer: staff-only pages seen = 0 (the filter is in the query).
MEASURE   retrieval and writing are scored apart: hit@3 100%, whole pass rate 61% with the stand-in writer.

A summary to check your answer against. The right-hand column is general knowledge about fine-tuning, not something measured here:

                      RAG                                       fine-tuning
where knowledge lives your documents, found at question time    inside the model's weights
update a fact         change the page, re-embed it (13 ms here)  retrain (hours or more; cost not measured here)
cite the source       yes: the chunk id travels with the text    no: it cannot say where a fact came from
per-user access       filter the query by role                   not possible: every user gets the same weights
good for              facts, documents, things that change       style, format, tone, a special task or behaviour
main risks            bad retrieval; the model ignores sources   it can still be wrong, and you cannot see why

Did your explanation use a number from your own runs? Notice the shape of the evidence: it is strong on updates, sources and permissions, which are the needs of this project, and silent on what fine-tuning is good at, which is not a need here. An explanation that says “RAG is better than fine-tuning” is too simple; one that says “RAG fits this problem because of updates, sources and permissions” is right.

5 · Fix the biggest gap 3 min

Look at the key points you did not tick. Pick the one that matters most and write a better sentence for it. Did you give the fair case for fine-tuning? Add it. Save your five sentences next to your earlier teach-backs.
