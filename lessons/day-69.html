Teach-back: why chunk size matters

30 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to explain in 5 sentences why chunk size matters, with real numbers.

1 · Warm-up: write down what you know 5 min

Close your notes. A colleague says: “Just use big chunks, so nothing is cut. Why would size matter?” Write what you would answer in two lines. Then write one situation where small chunks are better, and one where big chunks are.

Do not look anything up. Your guess is the most useful line you will write today.

2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: explain why chunk size matters for a search, in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note.

  • Who you are talking to: the colleague above, who wants big chunks.
  • Use these words: chunk, embedding, topic, tokens, overlap.
  • Rules: no notes, and give a number or an example from your own table.

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

1. A chunk is ... and a search can only return ...
2. If a chunk is too small, ... (for example E103 and E104 ...)
3. If a chunk is too big, ... because its vector ...
4. A bigger chunk also costs ..., because ...
5. So I would pick ... and check it by ...

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the key points and tick each one that your explanation said clearly. Be strict.

  1. A search returns chunks: it can only find what a chunk says, and the model can only read what you send. The chunk is the unit of everything after it.
  2. Too small: few words, so neighbouring topics look alike (E103 and E104), and the answer may be split over two chunks.
  3. Too big: many topics in one chunk, so the vector is a blur, and a chunk about something else can win.
  4. Cost: the tokens you send grow with the chunk size (58, 153 and 326 tokens for the top 3), and that is time, money and context-window space.
  5. Overlap and structure: overlap and cutting at sentences or headings soften the borders, at a price in extra tokens or the need for structure.
  6. Measure, do not guess: the best size depends on your documents and questions, so test on real questions and read the failures.
A model answer in 5 sentences

A search can only return whole chunks, so the size of a chunk decides what the model gets to read. If a chunk is too small, it has few words, so two neighbouring topics look alike: at 25 tokens the question about error E104 found the E103 chunk first. If a chunk is too big, it holds several topics and its vector becomes a blur: at 120 tokens a question about Wi-Fi found a chunk about something else, and sent 326 tokens instead of 153. In my five questions the middle size, 60 tokens, found the answer in the top 1 every time. But five questions on one guide is a hint, so I would pick a size by testing on my own questions and reading the failures.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

Check your explanation against it. Did you use the real numbers? One thing the table does not tell you: whether 60 is best for another document. It is five questions on a 546-token guide. An explanation that says “60 is best” claims too much; one that says “test on your own questions” is right.

5 · Fix the biggest gap 3 min

Look at the key points you did not tick. Pick the one that matters most and write a better sentence for it, using a number. Save your five sentences next to your earlier teach-backs.
