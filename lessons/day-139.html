Teach-back: SLI versus SLO

30 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to explain in 5 sentences what an SLI and an SLO are, with a number from your own system.

1 · Warm-up: write down what you know 5 min

Close your notes. A colleague says: “We have a dashboard with response times. Why would we need an SLO on top?” Write what you would answer in two lines. Then write one thing that an average response time can hide.

Do not look anything up. Your guess is the most useful line you will write today.

2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: explain SLI versus SLO in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note, with an example from your own assistant.

  • Who you are talking to: the colleague above.
  • Use these words: indicator, objective, error budget, good request, target.
  • Rules: no notes, and use a number from your own system.

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

1. An SLI is ... : a number that measures what ... feels, for example ... .
2. An SLO is ... : the target for that number, for example ... .
3. The error budget is ... , so with a 99% objective I may have ... bad requests in ... .
4. When the budget is used up, I ... (for example stop ... and fix ...).
5. A good SLI is ..., because ... ; a bad SLI is ... , because ... .

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the six key points and tick each one that your explanation said clearly. Be strict.

  1. SLI: a measurement of what the user feels (such as the share of requests that are good).
  2. SLO: the target for that measurement (such as at least 99% good). It is a decision, not a measurement.
  3. Error budget: what the target leaves. A 99% objective allows 1% bad requests.
  4. What is “good”: defined from the user’s side: not failed on your side, and fast enough. The caller’s mistakes (a 4xx) do not count against you.
  5. What it is for: a rule for action. When the budget is gone, you stop adding features and fix reliability; when plenty is left, you can take risks.
  6. Why not the average: an average hides the cold start, the slow tail and the failures; a share of good requests does not.
A model answer in 5 sentences

An SLI is a measurement of what the user feels: for my assistant, the share of requests that did not fail on my side and took under two seconds. An SLO is the target for it, for example at least 99 percent good, and the error budget is what is left, so 1 percent of requests may be bad. In my own numbers, one cold start of 3.6 seconds among 20 requests made the SLI 95 percent, so the objective was not met, and the fix was a warm-up at start, after which the SLI was 100 percent. The rule that comes with it is that when the budget is used up I fix reliability before I add features. I do not use the average response time for this, because an average of 187 milliseconds hid a single slow request that a share of good requests shows at once.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

4 · Look at the evidence 5 min

Do not trust the argument. Here is what your own system showed (real numbers from a rehearsal with a stand-in writer, not production):

SLI    share of /api/ask requests that were GOOD: status below 500 and 2000 ms or less (a 4xx is the caller's mistake)
SLO    at least 99% good

the 20-request run, with the first (cold) request of 3584 ms:     19 good of 20  ->  SLI 95.0%   SLO NOT met     (the budget of 1% is used 5 times over)
the 24-request run, after the warm-up:                             24 good of 24  ->  SLI 100.0%  SLO met         (error budget used: 0%)

cold start before the fix:  3447 ms and 596 ms          after the fix: 42 ms and 34 ms
the five numbers of the same run:  24 questions, 95.8% success, 10 ms average (median 8), 0.0049 USD (example prices), 1 failed

A summary to check your answer against:

                      an SLI                                   an SLO
what it is            a measurement                            a target for a measurement
example               share of good requests: 100.0%           at least 99% good over 7 days
who sets it           the metric you choose                    a decision: product, users and cost
what it is for        telling you how things ARE               telling you when things are NOT good enough
what follows          a chart                                   an alert, and a rule: when the budget is gone, fix before you add features

Look at what the evidence does: the SLI turned one slow request into a clear verdict (not met), the cause was known, and the fix was measured. An explanation that says “an SLO is a goal for speed” is too thin; one that says “an SLI measures good requests, an SLO is the target, and the budget tells me when to stop and fix” is right.

Look at the key points you did not tick. Pick the one that matters most and write a better sentence for it, using a number from your own system. Save your five sentences next to your earlier teach-backs.
