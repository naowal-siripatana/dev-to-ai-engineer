DAY 106 · LEVEL 8: EVALUATION · REVIEW

Self-test: evaluation words

1.5 hhands-onfree
Today you will be able to explain golden sets, metrics and judges in your own words, keep a results table, and plan week 16.

1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. Why does evaluation come before tuning? (Day 100)
  2. Name three ways a RAG assistant fails. (Day 100)
  3. What are the parts of a golden item, and why is the file validated? (Day 102)
  4. What do hit@3, rank and MRR measure? What is the MRR for ranks 1, 2 and none? (Day 102)
  5. Why score by hand before you trust a script? (Day 103)
  6. Why is 5 out of 5 on five easy questions not proof of quality? (Day 103)
  7. What is LLM-as-judge good for, and what are its risks? (Day 104)
  8. Why did hybrid search make “How do I install the monitor?” worse, and how did you find out? (Day 105)
  9. Why did “trust the keyword list less” fail as a fix? (Day 105)
  10. Which failures of your run belonged to the stand-in writer, and which to the pipeline? (Day 105)
Answers
  1. Without a score you cannot tell whether a change helped, and a change that fixes one question often breaks another.
  2. It finds the wrong page and answers confidently; it answers when it should refuse; it refuses when part of an answer exists.
  3. An id, the question, the expected sources (any one counts), optional phrases for the answer, and whether it should be refused. The file is validated so that a typo or a contradiction is caught as a file error and not mistaken for a pipeline failure.
  4. hit@3: is an expected page among the top 3. Rank: the position of the first expected page. MRR: the average of 1/rank (none is 0). For 1, 2 and none: (1 + 0.5 + 0) / 3 = 0.5.
  5. To know what the numbers mean, and to find bugs: if hand and script disagree, one of them is wrong.
  6. Five easy questions that you wrote first say little about the ones you did not write. The score is only as good as the set.
  7. Good for open-ended answers, where exact checks are too strict or cannot see completeness. Risks: cost per answer, variation between runs, biases (length, confidence, own style), and being wrong. Keep exact checks where they work and calibrate the judge against your own verdicts.
  8. The keyword list did not contain the install page (no stemming) and ranked the reset page first because of common words, and the fusion trusted it. The golden set showed it: hit@3 was 93% and q14 failed.
  9. It broke the E104 question, which needs the keyword list for an exact code. The table showed it at once.
  10. The five “answer lacks a phrase” failures came from the stand-in writer (first sentence only). The retrieval miss, the answered cost question and the refused waterproof question belong to the pipeline.

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.
