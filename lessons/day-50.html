1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. A model call is stateless. What does your code have to send for turn 2 of a conversation? (Day 39)
  2. What is a JSON Schema, and where do you get it from in your project? (Day 44)
  3. The model replies with “Sure! Here is the JSON:” and then the JSON. How does your helper cope, and what does it do when a value is wrong? (Day 44)
  4. Why is a provider feature that forces a schema not a reason to skip validation? (Day 44)
  5. Why does streaming feel faster when the total time is the same? What is the format of the events? (Day 46)
  6. The model fails halfway through a stream. Why can you not answer 502, and what do you do instead? (Day 46)
  7. Name two ways a model reply can be wrong that a schema catches, and one that it cannot. (Day 48)
  8. What are the two promises of /api/chat and /api/chat/stream, and why are they two endpoints? (Day 49)
Answers
  1. The whole history: the first question, the model’s first answer, and the new question, in the list of messages. The model keeps nothing between calls.
  2. A standard description of what a JSON object must look like: fields, types, allowed values, required fields. You get it from your Pydantic model with model_json_schema().
  3. A small function finds the first { and the last } and validates what is between them. If a value is wrong, it asks again with the exact problem, a few times at most, and raises an error if none is valid.
  4. It makes bad shapes rarer, but not impossible. Validation is your own guarantee, and it is cheap.
  5. The user sees the first words after a fraction of a second instead of waiting for the whole answer. The events are server-sent events: text/event-stream, with lines like data: ... and an optional event: name, each event ended by a blank line.
  6. The 200 status was sent before the first piece. You catch the error inside the generator and send an event: error, and a successful stream ends with event: done.
  7. Caught: a wrong value, a missing field, a wrong type, extra text, an answer cut off by the token limit. Not caught: a reply that has the right shape but is wrong in meaning, such as a low severity for an overheating device.
  8. /api/chat promises a schema-valid object (or a 502), which needs the whole answer. /api/chat/stream promises live text that ends with done or error. They are different promises, so they are separate endpoints.

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.
