1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. What is the difference between direct and indirect prompt injection? (Day 121)
  2. Why can a model not tell instructions from data? (Day 121)
  3. Which defence in the demo did not depend on the model, and why is it the strongest? (Day 121)
  4. Why an allow-list and not a deny-list? Why [0-9] and not \d? (Day 123)
  5. What belongs in an audit log, and what stays out? (Day 123)
  6. Why must an audit log never raise an error? (Day 112, 123)
  7. Why do we test with a hostile stand-in model? (Day 124)
  8. Which attack got through in your table, and why? (Day 124)
  9. Name the three levers against excessive agency, with the numbers from the four configurations. (Day 125)
Answers
  1. Direct: the user types the attack. Indirect: it is hidden in data that the assistant reads later (a page, an email), so an innocent user is the victim.
  2. The rules, the sources and the question are all text in one prompt, and the model reads them the same way.
  3. The role filter (least privilege): the staff secret was never in the customer’s prompt. A model cannot leak what it never received.
  4. A deny-list misses what you did not think of; an allow-list accepts only what is written down. \d also matches digits from other scripts (Arabic-Indic digits were accepted by the Pydantic pattern); [0-9] does not.
  5. In: role, event, time, every question and tool call with arguments, outcome and reason, every block. Out: secrets, keys, answer text and personal data you do not need; hide emails.
  6. A log that cannot be written (disk full, no permission) must not cost the user an answer.
  7. It assumes the model gives way, and tests whether the system around it still holds. You cannot prove that a real model resists.
  8. The forged header x-demo-role: staff: the role comes from something the caller writes, not from a login.
  9. Permissions (customer role: 0 closed), autonomy (a person who reads each request: 0 closed), blast radius (a budget of one risky call: 1 closed, against 7 when over-privileged).

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.
