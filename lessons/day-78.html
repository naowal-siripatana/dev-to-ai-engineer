1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. What does it mean for an answer to be grounded? Where do the rules and the sources go in the prompt? (Day 72)
  2. What is an invented citation, and how does your code find it? (Day 72)
  3. Why did the vector search miss the E104 page? (Day 74)
  4. How does BM25 weigh a rare word and a common word? (Day 74)
  5. Why do you fuse two searches by rank and not by adding the scores? (Day 74)
  6. When would you add a reranker, and what does it cost? (Day 74)
  7. Why do you check the best similarity before calling the writer, and why can it be high and the answer still fail? (Day 75)
  8. Name four ways an answer becomes a refusal in your pipeline. (Day 77)
  9. What does RAG reduce, and what does it not fix? (Day 76)
Answers
  1. The answer is built from the retrieved chunks only and every fact points to one of them. The rules go in the system prompt; the numbered sources and the question go in the user message.
  2. A citation in the answer that is not one of the retrieved chunks. The code finds every [id#n] with a regular expression and compares the ids with the retrieved set.
  3. An embedding captures meaning, and the error codes look alike to it; the exact characters E104 weigh little.
  4. It adds a weight for each query word that is in the chunk. A rare word (e104, in one chunk) weighs a lot; a common one (monitor) weighs little.
  5. The scores are in different units: a keyword score is much bigger than a similarity, so one list would decide. Ranks can be compared.
  6. When you have measured that the right chunk is among the candidates but not near the top. It costs time on every question.
  7. If nothing is close, the sources cannot help, and a writer might fill the gap. The score says something similar exists, not that the answer is in it; the right page may not have been retrieved.
  8. The best similarity is under the minimum; the writer says it found no answer; the answer cites a source that was not retrieved; the answer cites no source.
  9. It reduces made-up answers by giving the model sources, checking the citations and refusing without a source. It does not fix a search that misses the page, a misread source, or a wrong or old chunk.

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.

Now choose the three worst. Worst does not mean the longest or the rudest. Look for answers that would mislead a user: a wrong answer shown with a citation, a refusal when part of the answer exists, an answer that is right but incomplete. Write each one down with the type of failure, and what a test would check later:

notes/worst-answers.md

1. Question: How much does the monitor cost?
   Said    : The monitor has a two year warranty on manufacturing faults [faq-warranty#0].
   Should  : not found (the knowledge base has no price)
   Type    : an answer that does not match the question (the best similarity, 0.68, passed the gate)
   Test    : later, a set of questions that must be refused, with this one in it

2. Question: Is it waterproof?
   Said    : not found
   Should  : something like "keep it away from water spray; water damage is not covered by the warranty" [manual-safety#0] [faq-warranty#0]
   Type    : a refusal when part of the answer exists (best similarity 0.54, under the 0.6 gate)
   Test    : later, check the gate value on questions whose answers are only partly in the base

3. Question: What does error E104 mean?
   Said    : The storage is above 95 percent [error-e104#0].
   Should  : the storage is above 95 percent; export your readings and delete the old ones
   Type    : right source, incomplete answer (the writer kept only the first sentence)
   Test    : later, check that the action is in the answer
Why these three?

The price question is the most dangerous: the best similarity was 0.68, so it passed the gate, and the answer cites a real page, but the page does not answer the question. The waterproof question shows the other side of the gate: 0.54 is under 0.6, and part of the answer exists. The E104 answer is the least bad: right page, but the action is missing. Notice that the three failures have different causes (the writer, the gate, the writer), so one fix will not cover them. You will measure them in the evaluation weeks.

With a real model your list will be different. That is the point of doing it with your own pipeline.
