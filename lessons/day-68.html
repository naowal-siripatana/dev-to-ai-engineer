compare_sizes.py
  input : data/long/user-guide.md and these 5 questions, each with a phrase that must be in the answer chunk
  does  : for sizes 25, 60 and 120 tokens, cut the guide into whole sentences,

Hint: where to start

Loop over the sizes. For each: by_sentences(text, size), embed and normalise the chunks, then for each question take the dot product with the normalised question vector, sort in descending order, and test whether the phrase is in the chunk at the top, or in one of the first three.

Solution
from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.retrieval import normalize
from my_ai_project.strategies import by_sentences
from my_ai_project.tokens import count_tokens

QUESTIONS = [
    ("How often should I calibrate the sensor?", "once a month"),
    ("Which Wi-Fi band does the monitor support?", "2.4 GHz"),
    ("What does the error E104 mean?", "95 percent"),
    ("How do I silence the alarm?", "Silent"),
    ("How long does the battery last?", "three years"),
]
text = Path("data/long/user-guide.md").read_text(encoding="utf-8")
embedder = FastEmbedder()
query_vectors = normalize(embedder.embed([q for q, _ in QUESTIONS]))

print("size  chunks  top 1  top 3  tokens sent (top 3, average)")
for size in (25, 60, 120):
    chunks = by_sentences(text, size)
    vectors = normalize(embedder.embed(chunks))
    first = third = sent = 0
    for (question, marker), query in zip(QUESTIONS, query_vectors):
        order = (vectors @ query).argsort()[::-1]
        first += marker in chunks[order[0]]
        third += any(marker in chunks[i] for i in order[:3])
        sent += sum(count_tokens(chunks[i]) for i in order[:3])
    print(f"{size:>4}  {len(chunks):>6}  {first} of 5  {third} of 5  {sent / len(QUESTIONS):.0f}")
You should see
size  chunks  top 1  top 3  tokens sent (top 3, average)
  25      29  4 of 5  5 of 5  58
  60      11  5 of 5  5 of 5  153
120       5  4 of 5  5 of 5  326
Read the two failures

Two questions fail, one at each end. At size 25, for What does the error E104 mean?, the top 1 is the chunk about E103, a similar error. At size 120, for Which Wi-Fi band does the monitor support?, the top 1 is a chunk that starts “When the unit is ready, the status light turns green...”, which is about something else.

At 25 tokens, the E103 chunk is nearly the same sentence as the E104 chunk, and a tiny chunk has few words to tell them apart. At 120 tokens, one chunk holds several topics, so its vector is a blur, and a chunk about something else won. In the middle (60) all five are in the top 1. The cost: 153 tokens sent in the top 3, against 58 for size 25 and 326 for size 120.

This is five questions on one short guide: it is a hint, not a law. Your documents and your questions decide.

4 · Check yourself 5 min

  1. Which size gave the best top 1, and which sent the most tokens?
  2. Why can a very small chunk find the wrong answer?
  3. Why can a very big chunk find the wrong answer?
Answers
  1. Size 60 (5 of 5). Size 120 sent the most (326 tokens on average).
  2. It has few words, so two neighbouring topics look nearly the same to the embedding (E103 and E104).
  3. It holds many topics, so its vector is an average that matches no single question well.
