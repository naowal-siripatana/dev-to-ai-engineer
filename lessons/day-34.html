2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: explain attention with an analogy, in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note.

  • Who you are talking to: a friend who has used a chatbot but has no idea how it works.
  • Use these words: word, look, matter, mix, analogy. Your analogy must come from everyday life, not from computers.
  • Rules: no notes. A good analogy is one you made yourself.

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the six key points and tick each one that your explanation said clearly. Be strict.

  1. Attention lets each word look at the other words in the text, not only the one before it.
  2. Each word asks a question (the query), and the other words show what they hold (the keys). A match score says how well they fit.
  3. The scores become weights that add up to 1: like a share of attention, spread over the words.
  4. The word then takes a mix of the other words’ information (the values), mostly from the ones with the biggest weights.
  5. The questions and the matches are learned in training, and a model does it many times in parallel (many “heads”).
  6. Your analogy makes the idea easy to picture, and it says what is being matched and what is being mixed.
A model answer in 5 sentences

When a model reads a word, attention lets it look back at all the other words in the text. Each word asks a question, the other words show what they hold, and a match score says how well they fit. The scores turn into shares that add up to one, and the word takes a mix of the others, mostly from the best matches. It is like sitting at a noisy party with a question in your head: you hear everyone, but you listen mostly to the voices that help with your question. In a model, the questions and the matching are learned in training.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

4 · Look at the evidence 5 min

Do not trust the analogy until you see numbers move. This tiny script does one attention step by hand, in plain Python. The vectors are made up to show the mechanics (in a real model they are learned and much bigger). Each word has two numbers: how much it is a thing and how much it is a state. The word it asks: “which earlier word is a thing I can point to?” Save it as attention.py:

import math

# Made-up vectors, only to show the mechanics. Each token has two numbers: (is a thing, is a state).
tokens = ["the", "cable", "is", "loose", "so"]
keys = {
    "the": (0.0, 0.0),
    "cable": (1.0, 0.0),
    "is": (0.0, 0.1),
    "loose": (0.1, 0.9),
    "so": (0.0, 0.0),
}
query = (2.0, 0.0)  # the word "it" is asking: which earlier word is a thing I can point to?


def dot(a: tuple[float, float], b: tuple[float, float]) -> float:
    return a[0] * b[0] + a[1] * b[1]


def softmax(scores: list[float]) -> list[float]:
    exps = [math.exp(score) for score in scores]
    total = sum(exps)
    return [value / total for value in exps]


scores = [dot(query, keys[token]) for token in tokens]
weights = softmax(scores)
for token, score, weight in zip(tokens, scores, weights):
    print(f"{token:<6} score {score:4.1f}  attention {weight:.2f}")

Predict first: which word will get the most attention, and roughly how much?

You should see

is     score  0.0  attention 0.09
loose  score  0.2  attention 0.11

cable gets about two thirds of the attention, and the weights add up to 1. Now change the question: set query = (0.0, 2.0), so the word is asking for a state, and run it again:

You should see
the    score  0.0  attention 0.10
cable  score  0.0  attention 0.10
is     score  0.2  attention 0.12
loose  score  1.8  attention 0.59
so     score  0.0  attention 0.10

Now loose wins. Same words, different question, different attention. That is the whole idea. (If you try query = (6.0, 0.0), cable gets 99 percent: bigger scores make the choice sharper.)

This is one step, for one word, with two numbers. A real model does it for every word, with hundreds of numbers, in many layers.
