DAY 167 · LEVEL 15: PORTFOLIO · TEACH-BACK

Teach-back: a 3-minute project pitch

30 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to pitch your project with a problem, a proof, an honest limit and a next step.

1 · Warm-up: write down what you know 5 min

Close your notes. A friend asks: “what did you build in these six months, and why should I care?” Write what you would answer in two lines. Then write the one thing you are afraid they will ask next.

Do not look anything up. Your guess is the most useful line you will write today.

2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: pitch your project in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note. (Then, if you have time, the three-minute version below.)

  • Who you are talking to: someone who can read code but has never seen your project.
  • Use these words: problem, proof, decision, stand-in, next.
  • Rules: no notes, one number or one running thing as proof, and one sentence about what is not real.

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

1. I built ..., for people who ... , because ...
2. It works like this: ...
3. The proof that it runs: ... (a number, a command, a file)
4. One decision I made: ... instead of ..., because ...
5. What is not real: ... and what I would do next: ...
The three-minute version
A THREE-MINUTE PITCH, in the order of the six key points (about 6 sentences a minute is plenty)
0:00  the problem and who has it (one sentence)
0:20  what I built and how it fits (the diagram: say each box in one line)
0:50  the proof: one thing running, one number
1:30  a decision with its evidence, and what broke
2:10  what is not real, said calmly
2:40  what I would do next, and what I am looking for

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the six key points and tick each one that your pitch said clearly. Be strict.

  1. The problem and who has it: in one sentence a non-technical person understands.
  2. What you built: the parts in one breath (a page, a gateway, a service, a database), not a list of every library.
  3. Proof: one thing that runs, or one number from a real run. Not “it works well”.
  4. A decision: what you chose, what the alternative was, and the evidence that decided it (a comparison you ran).
  5. What is not real: stand-ins, things never run, samples too small to conclude from. Said once, calmly.
  6. What next: one concrete step, and what you are looking for (a job, feedback, a team).
A model answer in 5 sentences

I built a question-and-answer assistant for a made-up pump monitor, for support teams who answer the same questions all day and cannot afford a chatbot that invents things. It is a React page, a .NET gateway, a Python service and PostgreSQL with vector search, and it answers only from its own pages, cites them, and says so when it cannot find the answer. The proof is that a fresh clone runs from the README, 224 tests pass, and on my 18 test questions the stand-in writer passes 11 and a small local model between 5 and 17 depending on its size, which I report as a small sample. The default writer is a stand-in, and I never ran the Docker images or the GitHub pipeline myself. Next I want a real login and more questions, and I am looking for a junior AI engineer role where I can keep building like this.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

4 · Look at the evidence 5 min

Do not trust the pitch. For each claim, what backs it, and was it really run? The table is from one laptop with the stand-in writer, from a fresh clone. Predict first: which claims in your pitch have no run behind them?

"it answers from its own pages"          POST /api/ask: ... once a month [manual-calibration#0]    yes (stand-in writer)

"a stranger can run it"                  a fresh clone, the README steps, 5 gaps found and fixed   yes (without Docker)
"one command starts everything"          docker compose up                                          NO: the images were never built
"it is checked on every push"            the GitHub Actions workflow                                NO: never run on GitHub
"it works with a real model"             a 1.5B local model: 14 of 18 questions (4-bit)             yes, once; the hosted model: NO

Three claims have no real run behind them in this project: one command starting everything, the pipeline on every push, and the hosted model. A pitch can still say them, but only as “set up and checked as far as I could, never run”. If you cannot show it, either run it before the pitch or say it was not run.

5 · Fix the biggest gap 3 min

Look at the key points you did not tick. Pick the one that matters most and write a better sentence for it. Is there a claim in your pitch that you cannot back up? Cut it or mark it. Save your five sentences next to your earlier teach-backs.
