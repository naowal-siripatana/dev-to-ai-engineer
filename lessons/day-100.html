DAY 100 · LEVEL 8: EVALUATION

Why evaluation comes first

60-70 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to find three ways your assistant fails by reading real answers, and say why a score must come before tuning.

1 · Warm-up 3 min

Answer from memory, then open the answers.

  1. What did the human-in-the-loop step protect you from?
  2. Name one stop rule for an agent loop.
Answers
  1. A risky tool (one that changes something) running by mistake: a person approves the exact call first.
  2. A maximum number of steps, or stopping before a repeated action.

2 · Hook 2 min

You change one thing in your assistant: the chunk size, the prompt, the minimum similarity. How do you know whether it got better or worse? “I tried three questions and they looked fine”?

Guess first, then open

You do not know. A few good-looking answers prove little, and a change that fixes one question often breaks another. Evaluation means a fixed set of questions with expected results and a script that scores them, so that every change gets a number. It comes first: before you tune anything, you need a way to see whether tuning helps.

3 · The idea, in three small pieces 8 min

Piece 1: you cannot improve what you do not measure

Without a score, every change is a guess, and you will keep the changes that feel good. With a score, you can see that a change made one thing better and two things worse. In AI work the surprise is the normal case, because the parts depend on each other.

Piece 2: find the ways it fails, from real runs

Start by looking. Run a handful of different questions, read every answer, and write down the kinds of failure you see. For a RAG assistant there are three common ones: it finds the wrong page (and answers from it with confidence), it answers when it should refuse, and it refuses when it could have helped. Each kind needs its own check, and later its own fix.

Piece 3: one number cannot fix everything

The assistant has settings, such as the minimum similarity for answering. Moving one setting often trades one failure for another. That is exactly why you need many questions and a table: so that you see the trade, and do not fix one question while breaking three.

If you know another typed language (C# as the example): a golden set is a regression test suite for behaviour you cannot assert with an Assert.Equal. The score table is your CI result, and “a change that fixes one case and breaks another” is something you already know from refactoring without tests.
Capstone: Module 8 is one week. At the end of it, ai-knowledge-assistant has a golden set of 15 to 20 questions and a script that prints a score table. Every later change is judged by that table.

4 · Worked example 5 min

Six questions through the real pipeline (retrieval, the refusal rule, the grounded answer), as staff so that the error pages are included. failure_probe.py:

import os

from my_ai_project.access import allowed_kinds
from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.hybrid import KeywordIndex
from my_ai_project.rag import answer_question
from my_ai_project.simulated import SimulatedWriter
from my_ai_project.store import ChunkStore

QUESTIONS = [
    "How often should I calibrate the sensor?",
    "What does error E104 mean?",
    "How do I install the monitor?",
    "Where can I buy a coffee?",
    "How much does the monitor cost?",
    "Is it waterproof?",
]

store = ChunkStore(os.environ.get("DATABASE_URL", "postgresql://assistant:assistant@localhost:5433/assistant"))
embedder, keywords, writer = FastEmbedder(), KeywordIndex(store.all_chunks()), SimulatedWriter()   # the writer is a stand-in
for question in QUESTIONS:
    result = answer_question(question, embedder, store, keywords, writer, allowed_kinds=allowed_kinds("staff"))
    sources = ", ".join(s.citation for s in result.sources) or "no source"
    print(f"{question}\n   {result.text}\n   sources: {sources}" + (f"  (refused: {result.reason})" if result.refused else ""))
The writer is a stand-in. The answers come from the simulated writer of Day 72, which is not a real model: it writes the first sentence of the top page. The retrieval (which pages were found, and their similarity) is real. Keep this in mind: some failures belong to the retrieval, some to the writer.
You should see
How often should I calibrate the sensor?
   Calibrate the sensor once a month [manual-calibration#0].
   sources: [manual-calibration#0]
What does error E104 mean?
   The storage is above 95 percent [error-e104#0].
   sources: [error-e104#0]
How do I install the monitor?
   Hold the power button for 5 seconds until the light blinks [faq-reset#0].
   sources: [faq-reset#0]
Where can I buy a coffee?
   I could not find that in the knowledge base.
   sources: no source  (refused: best similarity 0.46 is under 0.6)
How much does the monitor cost?
   The monitor has a two year warranty on manufacturing faults [faq-warranty#0].
   sources: [faq-warranty#0]
Is it waterproof?
   I could not find that in the knowledge base.
   sources: no source  (refused: best similarity 0.54 is under 0.6)

Read it as a user would. Four of the six look plausible. Look harder:

  • Install: the question was about mounting the monitor and the answer is how to reset it, with a citation, in a calm voice. This is the worst kind: wrong, cited and confident.
  • Cost: the knowledge base has no price, and the assistant answered with the warranty.
  • Waterproof: it refused, but the knowledge base does say to keep the unit away from water spray, and that water damage is not covered.

5 · Try it 30 min

Work in my-ai-project, on a branch if you like (git switch -c evaluation). The database must be running, with the chunks of Day 67.

Step 1 Copy and run

Add failure_probe.py and run it. Match the six answers. Before you read my notes, write for each answer one word: good, wrong or doubtful.

Step 2 Change one thing

Add three questions of your own to the probe (one easy, one with an exact code, one that your knowledge base cannot answer). Predict first: which will fail? Run it and compare.

Step 3 Break it on purpose

Change the minimum similarity of answer_question (the min_score argument, now 0.6) to 0.5, then to 0.7. Predict first: which of the six answers change? Does one number fix the failures?

Step 4 Your turn

Write notes/failures.md: three ways your assistant fails, each with a real question from a run, what it said, what it should have said, the type of failure, and how a script could detect it automatically. This is the plan for the whole week.

Commit: git add . and git commit -m "Find three ways the assistant fails".

6 · Stuck? anytime

  • Different answers from mine: your knowledge base or your chunking may differ. That is fine; use your own results.
  • Every answer is “not found”: check that you ask as staff (the error pages are for staff) and that ingest.py was run.
  • You cannot tell good from wrong: open the page the answer cites and read it next to the question. That is the whole skill.
Solution: Step 1 (my labels)

Calibration: good. E104: good (though only the first sentence; the action is missing). Install: wrong. Coffee: good (a correct refusal). Cost: wrong (it should have refused). Waterproof: doubtful (a refusal, where a partial answer exists).

Solution: Step 3
You should see
min_score  calibrate  E104       install    coffee   cost       waterproof
0.5        answered   answered   answered   refused  answered   refused
0.6        answered   answered   answered   refused  answered   refused
0.7        answered   refused    answered   refused  refused    refused

At 0.5 nothing changes: the waterproof question is still refused, but by the writer (no word of the question is in the page), not by the gate. At 0.7 the cost question is refused (good), but the E104 question is refused too (bad: its best similarity is 0.63). One number trades one failure for another, and the install failure is not about this number at all (the right page was never retrieved). You need a table of many questions to see this.

Solution: Step 4 (a model)
notes/failures.md

1. WRONG PAGE, SAID WITH CONFIDENCE
   Question : How do I install the monitor?
   Said     : Hold the power button for 5 seconds until the light blinks [faq-reset#0].
   Should   : Mount the monitor on a flat wall ... [manual-install#0]
   Type     : retrieval miss: the right page was not in the top 3
   Detect   : the expected source is not among the retrieved pages

2. ANSWERED WHEN IT SHOULD REFUSE
   Question : How much does the monitor cost?
   Said     : The monitor has a two year warranty on manufacturing faults [faq-warranty#0].
   Should   : I could not find that in the knowledge base.
   Type     : a real citation to a page that does not answer the question
   Detect   : the question is marked 'should refuse' and the assistant answered

3. REFUSED WHEN PART OF THE ANSWER EXISTS
   Question : Is it waterproof?
   Said     : I could not find that in the knowledge base.
   Should   : keep it away from water spray; water damage is not covered by the warranty
   Type     : a refusal where a partial answer was possible
   Detect   : the question is marked 'answerable' and the assistant refused

7 · Check yourself 5 min

  1. Why does evaluation come before tuning?
  2. Name the three ways a RAG assistant fails, from today’s run.
  3. Why can raising the minimum similarity make things worse?
Answers
  1. Without a score you cannot tell whether a change helped, and a change that fixes one question often breaks another.
  2. It finds the wrong page and answers confidently; it answers when it should refuse; it refuses when part of an answer exists.
  3. It refuses more questions, including good ones: at 0.7 the correct E104 answer is refused together with the cost question.
