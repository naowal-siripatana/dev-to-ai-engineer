2 · Hook 2 min

You have one long manual and you must cut it into pieces for search. Cut it every 80 tokens, like slicing bread? Or at sentences? Or at the headings the author wrote? Does it matter?

Guess first, then open

It matters. A piece that starts in the middle of a sentence, or loses its heading, may not make sense on its own, and a search can only return what a piece says. There is no best way for every document, so today you cut one document three ways and look at the pieces.

3 · The idea, in three small pieces 8 min

Piece 1: fixed windows are simple, and blind

A fixed window cuts the text every N tokens, whatever the text says. It is easy and predictable, but it cuts through sentences and headings. To soften that, you let neighbouring windows overlap: each one repeats the last few tokens of the one before. Overlap helps a sentence that falls on a border, and it costs extra tokens, because the overlapping text is stored and searched twice.

Piece 2: cut where the text already breaks

Text has natural breaks: sentences and paragraphs. Packing whole sentences into chunks of at most N tokens (your splitter from Day 35) never cuts a sentence. Cutting by sections (a heading and the text under it) keeps a topic together and keeps the heading, which is a very good clue for search. A section that is too long falls back to sentences.

Piece 3: compare strategies on your own data

No strategy is best for all documents. You choose by looking at the pieces (do they make sense alone?), by their sizes, and by testing whether a search finds the answers. Small numbers of questions only give hints, but looking at real chunks is always worth the five minutes.

If you know another typed language (C# as the example): fixed windows with overlap are a sliding window over a stream (like Chunk and Window in LINQ libraries), and cutting by headings is a Split on a delimiter. The trade-off is the usual one: the dumb approach is predictable, the structure-aware one needs the structure to exist.
Capstone: your knowledge base is short pages today, but real manuals are long. This week you choose how ai-knowledge-assistant will cut them, and on Saturday you index the chunks with metadata.

4 · Worked example 5 min

Read it first. We use one longer document, a made-up user guide for the pump monitor. Download user-guide.md (about 460 words, 8 sections) and save it as data/long/user-guide.md. It is 546 tokens long. Three functions in src/my_ai_project/strategies.py:

import re

from my_ai_project.chunking import split_text
from my_ai_project.tokens import ENCODING


def fixed_windows(text: str, size: int, overlap: int) -> list[str]:
    """Cut the token list into windows of `size` tokens, each starting `size - overlap` tokens after the last."""
    if not 0 <= overlap < size:
        raise ValueError("overlap must be at least 0 and smaller than size")
    ids = ENCODING.encode(text)
    step = size - overlap
    return [ENCODING.decode(ids[start : start + size]).strip() for start in range(0, max(len(ids) - overlap, 1), step)]


def by_sentences(text: str, max_tokens: int) -> list[str]:
    """Whole sentences packed into chunks of at most `max_tokens` tokens (the splitter from Day 35)."""
    return split_text(re.sub(r"^#+ ", "", text, flags=re.MULTILINE), max_tokens)


def by_sections(text: str, max_tokens: int) -> list[str]:
    """One chunk per section (a heading and its text). A section that is too long is cut by sentences."""
    chunks: list[str] = []
    for section in re.split(r"\n(?=#+ )", text.strip()):
        heading, _, body = section.partition("\n")
        title = heading.lstrip("# ").strip()
        body = body.strip()
        if not body:
            continue   # the document title has no text of its own
        pieces = split_text(body, max_tokens - len(ENCODING.encode(title)) - 2)
        chunks.extend(f"{title}: {piece}" for piece in pieces)
    return chunks

A script that cuts the guide three ways and shows sizes and borders. compare_strategies.py:

from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.strategies import by_sections, by_sentences, fixed_windows
from my_ai_project.tokens import count_tokens

text = Path("data/long/user-guide.md").read_text(encoding="utf-8")
strategies = {
    "fixed windows (80, overlap 20)": fixed_windows(text, 80, 20),
    "whole sentences (max 80)": by_sentences(text, 80),
    "sections (max 120)": by_sections(text, 120),
}
for name, chunks in strategies.items():
    sizes = [count_tokens(chunk) for chunk in chunks]
    mid_cut = sum(1 for chunk in chunks if chunk[-1] not in ".!?")
    print(f"{name:<32} {len(chunks):>2} chunks, {min(sizes)} to {max(sizes)} tokens, {mid_cut} end in the middle of a sentence")

print()
for name, chunks in strategies.items():
    print(f"--- {name}: chunks 3 and 4")
    for chunk in chunks[2:4]:
        print("   |", chunk.replace("\n", " ")[:110])

6 · Stuck? anytime

  • The chunk counts differ from the lesson: check that your user-guide.md is the downloaded file (546 tokens) and that the sizes (80, 20, 120) are the ones in the script.
  • A window is 81 or 82 tokens: decoding and trimming spaces can move a token. The test allows 82.
  • The sections strategy gives one huge chunk: a section longer than the limit must fall back to sentences. Check that you pass the limit minus the heading size.
  • “ModuleNotFoundError ... chunking”: the splitter from Day 35 must be in src/my_ai_project/.
Hint: Step 4

For each strategy: embed all the chunks once, embed the question, take the dot product of normalised vectors, sort, and look at whether the phrase (for example 2.4 GHz) is inside the first chunk, or inside one of the first three.

Solution: Step 4
from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.retrieval import normalize
from my_ai_project.strategies import by_sections, by_sentences, fixed_windows

QUESTIONS = [
    ("How often should I calibrate the sensor?", "once a month"),
    ("Which Wi-Fi band does the monitor support?", "2.4 GHz"),
    ("What does the error E104 mean?", "95 percent"),
]
text = Path("data/long/user-guide.md").read_text(encoding="utf-8")
embedder = FastEmbedder()
strategies = {
    "fixed windows (80, overlap 20)": fixed_windows(text, 80, 20),
    "whole sentences (max 80)": by_sentences(text, 80),
    "sections (max 120)": by_sections(text, 120),
}
for name, chunks in strategies.items():
    vectors = normalize(embedder.embed(chunks))
    first, third = 0, 0
    for question, marker in QUESTIONS:
        scores = vectors @ normalize(embedder.embed([question]))[0]
        order = scores.argsort()[::-1]
        first += marker in chunks[order[0]]
        third += any(marker in chunks[i] for i in order[:3])
    print(f"{name:<32} answer in the top 1: {first} of {len(QUESTIONS)}, in the top 3: {third} of {len(QUESTIONS)}")

A model answer for the table

Fixed windows: no whole sentences; the heading is kept only by luck; cost grows with the overlap. Whole sentences: whole sentences; the heading is lost; no extra cost. By sections: whole sentences (inside a section); the heading is kept as a prefix; no extra cost, but it needs headings in the document. For this guide, sections: it has clear headings, each section is about one topic, and they fit in 120 tokens.

Template: tests
from pathlib import Path

import pytest

from my_ai_project.strategies import by_sections, by_sentences, fixed_windows
from my_ai_project.tokens import count_tokens

TEXT = Path("data/long/user-guide.md").read_text(encoding="utf-8")


def test_fixed_windows_never_go_over_the_size() -> None:
    chunks = fixed_windows(TEXT, 80, 20)
    assert all(count_tokens(chunk) <= 82 for chunk in chunks)   # decoding and stripping can move a token or two
    assert len(chunks) > 5


def test_fixed_windows_overlap_by_a_few_words() -> None:
    chunks = fixed_windows("one two three four five six seven eight nine ten", 6, 2)
    assert chunks[0].split()[-1] in chunks[1]


def test_an_overlap_as_big_as_the_size_is_rejected() -> None:
    with pytest.raises(ValueError, match="smaller than size"):
        fixed_windows(TEXT, 50, 50)


def test_sentence_chunks_end_with_a_full_stop() -> None:
    assert all(chunk.endswith(".") for chunk in by_sentences(TEXT, 80))


def test_each_section_chunk_starts_with_its_heading() -> None:
    chunks = by_sections(TEXT, 120)
    assert chunks[0].startswith("Overview: ")
    assert any(chunk.startswith("Calibration: ") for chunk in chunks)
    assert all(count_tokens(chunk) <= 120 for chunk in chunks)

7 · Check yourself 5 min

  1. What does overlap do for fixed windows, and what does it cost?
  2. Why can a chunk without its heading be harder to find?
  3. Why is it risky to choose a strategy from three test questions?
Answers
  1. It repeats the end of one window at the start of the next, so a sentence on a border is whole in at least one chunk. It costs extra tokens, because the repeated text is stored and searched twice.
  2. The heading says what the text is about. Without it the chunk may be about “the menu” or “two minutes” with nothing to say which task it belongs to.
  3. Three questions are too few to see a real difference, and one question changing sides moves the result a lot. Use more questions, and look at the chunks too.
