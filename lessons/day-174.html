DAY 174 · LEVEL 13: SHOW YOUR WORK · TEACH-BACK

Teach-back: rest, pack and prepare questions

30 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to say calmly what is not real in your project, and name the questions you will ask at the workshop.

1 · Warm-up: write down what you know 5 min

Close your notes. Someone at the workshop asks you: “what did not work?” Write what you would answer in two lines, calmly, with a real example. Then write a question about your own project that you are afraid to hear.

Do not look anything up. Your guess is the most useful line you will write today.

2 · Explain it 10 min

Your task: prepare what you will say about what is not real, and the questions you will ask, in 5 sentences or a 60-second voice note.

  • Who you are talking to: a room of people who build things, who will respect honesty.
  • Use these words: stand-in, not run, broke, I don’t know yet, question.
  • Rules: no notes, one real example of something that broke, and one thing you do not know yet (said calmly, with how you would find out).

Write it or record it now. A rough first try is exactly right.

Stuck? Sentence starters

Use these if you freeze:

1. What is not real in my project is ..., and I say it because ...
2. The one number I can show is ..., from ...
3. Something that broke was ..., and I found it by ...
4. A question I cannot answer yet is ...; I would find out by ...
5. At the workshop I want to ask others: ..., ..., ...

3 · Check it against the key points 8 min

Read the six key points and tick each one that your answer said clearly. Be strict.

  1. The honest limit: what is a stand-in, never run or too small to conclude from, in one calm sentence.
  2. One number you can show: from a run you can repeat, with where it came from.
  3. Something that broke: a real example, how you found it, what you changed.
  4. A question you cannot answer yet: “I don’t know yet; I would find out by ...”. It is a strength, not a gap.
  5. Three questions to ask: about your work, about theirs, about what to learn next.
  6. Packed and rested: the laptop works offline, the recording is a file, the last change was made yesterday.
A model answer in 5 sentences

What is not real in my project is the writer: the default is a stand-in, and I never ran the Docker images or the pipeline on GitHub myself, and I say it because every other claim is more believable for it. The one number I can show is that on my 18 test questions the stand-in passes 11 and a small local model between 5 and 17 depending on its size, from one run each. Something that broke was a fresh clone that could not fill a new database, and I found it by following my own README as a stranger. A question I cannot answer yet is how it behaves with real users; I would find out by giving it to ten of them. At the workshop I want to ask others what they would check first in my project, what was hardest for them, and what they would want to see if they were hiring.

Count your ticks. 6: very clear. 3 to 5: good, you found your gaps. 0 to 2: also fine, the next step fills them in.

4 · Look at the evidence 5 min

Do not trust that you are ready. Run one command that checks everything that can be checked on your machine. This is the real output on the capstone, from a fresh clone, with the system running. Predict first: how long will all of it take, and which checks cannot be in it?

You should see
bash scripts/preflight.sh                     (run for real, the system running, from the top of the repository)
diagram matches compose.yaml      ok
no obvious secret in Git          ok
python: types                     ok
python: tests                     ok
gateway: tests                    ok
page: build                       ok
demo path (system must be up)     ok
PREFLIGHT OK                      (16 s in all)
(not in it, because it cannot run here: the Docker images, the Compose stack, the GitHub workflow)

It takes seconds, so there is no reason not to run it the night before and the morning of. What it cannot check is just as important: the pieces you never ran (the images, the Compose stack, the pipeline) and you. Packing and rest fill in the rest:

PACK (the night before)
on the laptop    the repository cloned and working OFFLINE (the embedding model on disk in a folder that survives a restart)
                 the system started once from the README with internet, then once without
                 the recorded demo (a file, not a link), and screenshots of each beat
                 the README and the diagram open in tabs
with me          charger, the right cable or adapter for the screen, a phone as a hotspot, a notebook and a pen
                 water, something to eat
decided          the first two sentences of my talk, the honest sentence, and the three questions I will ask
NOT doing        any new feature, any upgrade of a tool, any change to the system after tonight's last check
REST (general practice, not a measurement)
the day before     one last run of the preflight; then stop. A change made tonight is a change you cannot test.
the night before   pack, set two alarms, put the laptop on the charger, go to bed at a time you can keep
the morning        eat; start the system first; run the demo check; ask one throwaway question; then do not touch it again
THREE GOOD QUESTIONS TO ASK AT A WORKSHOP (write yours)
about my work      "What would you check first in my project if it were yours?"        (they find what you cannot see)
about theirs       "What was the hardest thing to get working, and what did you do?"   (you learn a technique, and they enjoy answering)
about next         "If you were hiring for this skill, what would you want to see?"    (you learn what to polish next)

5 · Fix the biggest gap 3 min

Look at the key points you did not tick. Pick the one that matters most and write a better sentence for it. Is your “I don’t know yet” calm? Say it aloud once. Then do the packing list: tick it off, and stop working.
