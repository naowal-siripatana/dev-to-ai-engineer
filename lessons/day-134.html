1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. What are logs, metrics and traces, and what connects them? (Day 128)
  2. Why must you check the shape of a request id that comes from outside? (Day 128, 131)
  3. Which things do you record for an LLM call, and which do you keep out? (Day 128)
  4. What is the formula for the cost of a request, and which two parts of it were not real in your run? (Day 130)
  5. What is the difference between NULL and 0 for tokens? Give an example of each. (Day 130)
  6. Why does the average latency lie, and what do you show next to it? (Day 130, 133)
  7. Why must recording metrics never raise an error? (Day 130)
  8. Name the first three things you would check at 2 a.m., and say what a baseline is for. (Day 132)
  9. Why is a refusal counted as a success, and a 422 as a failure? (Day 133)
Answers
  1. Logs say what happened, metrics are numbers across many requests, a trace follows one request through several services. The request id connects them.
  2. A header is user input: a line break in it could forge extra lines in your log. Accept only a safe shape (letters, digits, dot, dash, underscore, up to 64 characters), and replace anything else.
  3. Record the route, role, latency, model, tokens, cost, outcome, source ids and any block. Keep out secrets and the full text of prompts and answers (or hide private strings).
  4. cost = (input tokens x input price + output tokens x output price) / 1,000,000. The tokens were estimates with tiktoken (not billed), and the prices were examples.
  5. NULL means not measured (a failed request); 0 means measured and zero (a question refused before the model was called). Mixing them makes averages lie.
  6. One very slow request (the cold start, 3584 ms) pulled the average to 187 ms, while the median was 8 ms. Show the median and the worst case beside it.
  7. Metrics are a by-product: if the database is down, the row is lost, but the user’s request must not fail.
  8. Is it up, is anything failing, is it slow. A baseline tells you what is normal, so a number means something.
  9. A refusal is a correct result (the assistant says it does not know). An error is a failure to give any result (here a user’s mistake, a 4xx).

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.
