Problem framing and requirements for AI

60-70 minhands-onfree
Today you will be able to frame a problem, write three testable requirements and one success criterion with numbers, and measure where you are today.

1 · Warm-up 3 min

Answer from memory, then open the answers.

  1. What is the first thing to do when you review code from an agent?
  2. Why does the instructions file have a “done means” part?
Answers
  1. Read the diff before the summary, and run the feature with wrong input.
  2. It sets the standard the agent holds itself to: tests, types, the score table, the diff and what was not tested.

2 · Hook 2 min

Someone says: “We need an AI chatbot for support.” Before you write any code, what four things must you find out, and why is “smart and fast” not a requirement?

Guess first, then open

Who is it for, what job does it do, what hurts today, and what is not its job. And a requirement must be testable: someone must be able to run a check and say “yes, done” or “no, not yet”. “Smart” and “fast” cannot be checked, so they cannot be built or finished.

3 · The idea, in three small pieces 8 min

Piece 1: frame the problem before the solution

Write down who has the problem, the job they want done, the pain today, and what is not your job (non-goals). Non-goals matter as much as goals: they protect you from building a refund bot when you set out to build a manual finder. Notice that nothing here says “AI”. AI is one possible answer to the job, chosen later (Wednesday).

Piece 2: requirements you can test

A good requirement says one thing and says how to check it: a yes or no, or a number. Write the check next to the requirement. If you cannot say how to check it, rewrite it until you can. For an AI system there are usually requirements about the answers (cited, refused when unknown), about who may see what, and about speed and cost. Your golden set (Module 8) is the tool that checks the first kind.

Piece 3: one success criterion, with numbers

Besides the requirements, write one sentence that says when the project is a success, with numbers, on a set you will actually run. Then write where you are today. The gap between the two is your plan. A criterion that you cannot measure today (for example “with the real model” before you have run one) is fine, as long as you say that it is not measured yet.

If you know another typed language (C# as the example): requirements with a check are acceptance criteria in a user story, and the golden set is your acceptance test suite. “Non-goals” is the “out of scope” line of a good ticket, and the success criterion is the definition of done for the whole feature.
Capstone: Module 10 ends with an ADR for ai-knowledge-assistant. A decision record is only as good as the problem it solves, so this week starts here.

4 · Worked example 5 min

A vague request, and why it fails:

'A chatbot for support that is smart and fast and answers everything.'
   smart?  fast?  everything?  Nobody can test this, so nobody can say it is done.

Turning a vague sentence into a testable one:

vague                                     testable
'answers should be accurate'              'every answered golden question cites an expected page'
'it should be fast'                       'retrieval p95 under 50 ms (model call not included)'
'it should be secure'                     'a customer sees 0 staff-only pages in 18 questions'
'it should not make things up'            'every should_refuse question is refused'

The framing of the PumpGuard assistant, as a real docs/problem.md. The numbers in it come from real runs, shown below:

docs/problem.md

WHO        Customers of the PumpGuard monitor, and the support staff who help them.
JOB        Find the answer to 'what does this mean / how do I do this' in the manuals, FAQs and error pages,
           without waiting for a person.
PAIN       Support staff answer the same 20 questions every week, and customers read long manuals.
NOT        Not a replacement for support staff. Not for refunds, prices or anything not in the pages.
           Not available in languages other than English (for now).

REQUIREMENTS (each one can be tested)
R1  Answers come from the knowledge base and cite their sources.
      check: every answered golden question cites an expected page            (citation_ok in run_golden.py)
R2  When the knowledge base has no answer, the assistant says so and does not guess.
      check: every golden question marked should_refuse is refused            (refusal accuracy)
R3  A customer never receives content from staff-only pages.
      check: 0 staff-only pages seen in 18 questions asked as a customer      (leak_check.py)
R4  Retrieval is fast enough not to matter next to the model call.
      check: retrieval p95 under 50 ms on this knowledge base                 (measure_latency.py)

SUCCESS CRITERION (one sentence, with numbers, on the golden set, with the real model)
  'On the 18 golden questions, pass rate at least 85%, hit@3 at least 95%, and no question that should be refused is answered;
   and R3 holds.'
TODAY   pass 11/18 (61%) with the stand-in writer, hit@3 100%, refusals 89%, R3 holds, retrieval p95 7.6 ms.
        The gap to the criterion is the work. It is not measured with a real model yet.

Two of the four checks are new scripts. The speed check, measure_latency.py (it times each stage of the retrieval for the 18 golden questions, three runs each, after the model has loaded; no model call and no writer are included):

import os
import statistics
import time
from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.access import allowed_kinds
from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.golden import load_golden
from my_ai_project.hybrid import KeywordIndex, reciprocal_rank_fusion
from my_ai_project.store import ChunkStore

store = ChunkStore(os.environ.get("DATABASE_URL", "postgresql://assistant:assistant@localhost:5433/assistant"))
chunks = store.all_chunks()
items = load_golden(Path("data/golden.jsonl"), {chunk.doc_id for chunk in chunks})
embedder, keywords = FastEmbedder(), KeywordIndex(chunks)
kinds = allowed_kinds("staff")
embedder.embed(["warm up"])   # the first call loads the model: do not count it

stages: dict[str, list[float]] = {"embed the question": [], "vector search (pgvector)": [], "keyword search (BM25)": [], "fusion": [], "retrieval in all": []}
for _ in range(3):
    for item in items:
        t0 = time.perf_counter()
        vector = embedder.embed([item.question])[0]
        t1 = time.perf_counter()
        by_meaning = store.search(vector, top_k=10, allowed_kinds=kinds)
        t2 = time.perf_counter()
        by_words = keywords.search(item.question, top_k=10, allowed_kinds=kinds)
        t3 = time.perf_counter()
        reciprocal_rank_fusion([by_meaning, by_words])
        t4 = time.perf_counter()
        for name, start, end in zip(stages, (t0, t1, t2, t3, t0), (t1, t2, t3, t4, t4)):
            stages[name].append((end - start) * 1000)

print(f"{len(items)} questions x 3 runs, {len(chunks)} chunks   (milliseconds)")
print(f"{'stage':<28}{'median':>8}{'p95':>8}{'max':>8}")
for name, values in stages.items():
    ordered = sorted(values)
    p95 = ordered[int(0.95 * (len(ordered) - 1))]
    print(f"{name:<28}{statistics.median(values):>8.1f}{p95:>8.1f}{max(values):>8.1f}")

stage                         median     p95     max
embed the question               2.9     3.8     4.0
vector search (pgvector)         3.5     4.2    17.7
keyword search (BM25)            0.0     0.1     0.1
fusion                           0.0     0.0     0.0
retrieval in all                 6.5     7.6    21.0

Most of the 6.5 ms is embedding the question and the database round trip; the keyword search and the fusion cost almost nothing. A real model call takes far longer, so retrieval is not the thing to optimise. That is a result you could not have known without measuring. The permission check, leak_check.py (it asks every golden question as a customer and counts the staff-only pages that were retrieved or cited):

import os
from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.access import allowed_kinds
from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.golden import load_golden
from my_ai_project.hybrid import KeywordIndex, hybrid_search
from my_ai_project.rag import answer_question
from my_ai_project.simulated import SimulatedWriter
from my_ai_project.store import ChunkStore

store = ChunkStore(os.environ.get("DATABASE_URL", "postgresql://assistant:assistant@localhost:5433/assistant"))
chunks = store.all_chunks()
items = load_golden(Path("data/golden.jsonl"), {chunk.doc_id for chunk in chunks})
embedder, keywords, writer = FastEmbedder(), KeywordIndex(chunks), SimulatedWriter()   # the writer is a stand-in

staff_only = {chunk.doc_id for chunk in chunks if chunk.kind == "error"}
leaks = 0
for item in items:
    found = hybrid_search(item.question, embedder, store, keywords, top_k=10, allowed_kinds=allowed_kinds("customer"))
    answer = answer_question(item.question, embedder, store, keywords, writer, allowed_kinds=allowed_kinds("customer"))
    seen = {f.doc_id for f in found} | {s.doc_id for s in answer.sources}
    leaks += len(seen & staff_only)
print(f"{len(items)} questions asked as a customer, {len(staff_only)} staff-only pages: staff pages seen = {leaks}")
You should see
18 questions asked as a customer, 7 staff-only pages: staff pages seen = 0

5 · Try it 30 min

Work in my-ai-project, on a branch if you like (git switch -c docs/problem).

Step 1 Frame the problem

Write docs/problem.md with who, job, pain and non-goals for your assistant. Keep each to one or two lines. Write at least two non-goals.

Step 2 Three requirements and their checks

Write three requirements. Next to each, write the check: the script or the number. If a requirement has no check, rewrite it with the table “vague / testable” as a guide.

Step 3 Measure where you are

Run the checks you can run today: run_golden.py, and the two scripts above (add measure_latency.py and leak_check.py). Predict first: will your retrieval p95 be under 50 ms? Will your customer see 0 staff pages? Write the numbers in the file.

Step 4 The success criterion

Write one sentence with numbers, and then a line “Today: ...” with your real numbers. Say which of them are not measured yet. Then answer: which one gap would you close first, and why?

Commit: git add docs/problem.md and git commit -m "Frame the problem: requirements and a success criterion".

6 · Stuck? anytime

  • You cannot think of a check: ask “what would I run to prove it is wrong?” If nothing, the requirement is too vague. Split it.
  • Too many requirements: keep the three that would hurt most to break. Others can wait.
  • Your numbers are far from the criterion: good. That is the plan. Do not lower the criterion to match the numbers.
  • You have no real model yet: say so in the file. A number from a stand-in is a real number about the stand-in.
Solution: Step 3 (what to expect)

The retrieval time is a few milliseconds on a small knowledge base, so the requirement is easy to meet today and worth keeping only as a guard that tells you if a change makes it slow. The golden pass rate is the number that is far from the criterion. And the permission check should be exactly 0: any other number is a bug to fix first.

Solution: Step 4 (the gap)

In the example, the gap is between 61% and 85% on the golden set. The failures behind it were: five that belong to the stand-in writer (it writes only the first sentence), one wrong answer to the price question, and one refusal of the waterproof question. Only a real model can show how much of the gap is the writer. So the first step of the plan is to run the golden set with a real model, not to tune the retrieval, which is already at 100% hit@3.
