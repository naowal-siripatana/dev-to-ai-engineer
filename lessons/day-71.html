Self-test: chunking and retrieval

1.5 hhands-onfree
Today you will be able to explain chunking and retrieval in your own words, start the system from cold, and plan week 11.

1 · Self-test 25 min

Close your notes. Answer each question in your own words, and write a confidence number next to it: 1 = guessing, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain. Then open the answers and mark each one right, partly right or wrong.

  1. What are fixed windows, and what does overlap do for them and cost? (Day 65)
  2. Why does cutting by sections help a search? When does it not work? (Day 65)
  3. What are the three steps of the retrieval pipeline? (Day 67)
  4. How do you add a column to a table that has data, and what must you do after it? (Day 67)
  5. Why does an unknown filter value raise an error and not return an empty list? (Day 67)
  6. What happened at chunk size 25 and at 120 in your comparison, and why? (Day 68)
  7. Why is five questions not enough to pick a size for good? (Day 65, Day 68)
  8. What does /api/search return, and why does each result have a citation? (Day 70)
  9. Why is a database that is down a 503 and a wrong kind a 422? (Day 70)
Answers
  1. Fixed windows cut the text every N tokens. Overlap repeats the end of one window at the start of the next, so a sentence on a border is whole in one chunk, and it costs extra tokens (the repeated text is stored and searched twice).
  2. It keeps a topic together and keeps the heading, which is a clue for the search. It needs headings in the document, and a section longer than the limit must fall back to sentences.
  3. Embed the question, search the store for the nearest chunks, filter by what you know.
  4. ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS is a migration. Then run the ingest again to fill the new columns.
  5. An empty list looks like “nothing exists”, so a typo would go unseen. An error shows it at once.
  6. At 25, the question about E104 found the E103 chunk first (tiny chunks of similar errors look alike). At 120, one chunk held several topics and a chunk about something else won, and 326 tokens were sent. At 60 all five were found in the top 1.
  7. Too few questions to see a real difference, and one question changing sides moves the result a lot. Look at the failures, and test on your own questions.
  8. The top chunks with the question, the kind, the text, the similarity and a citation like [error-e101#0]. The citation lets a later answer show where a claim came from.
  9. A 422 means the caller sent something wrong. A 503 means the service cannot work right now.

Read your numbers. If you were sure (3) and wrong, that is the most valuable thing you found. Circle those.
