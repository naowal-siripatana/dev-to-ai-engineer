Project Lab 17: the ADR

2.5 hhands-onfree
Today you will be able to write an architecture decision record with evidence for every reason, a fair alternative, and one thing you chose not to build.

1 · Warm-up 5 min

Answer from memory, then open the answers.

  1. Where does the knowledge live in RAG, and where in fine-tuning?
  2. Name two things RAG can do that fine-tuning cannot.
Answers
  1. RAG: in your documents, found at question time. Fine-tuning: in the model’s weights.
  2. Cite the source of a fact, and filter what each user may see. (Also: update a fact in milliseconds, without training.)

2 · The goal 5 min

This is the Module 10 checkpoint: one ADR (architecture decision record) in your repository. An ADR is a short document that records one decision: the situation, what you decided, why, what else you considered, and what you did not build. By the end:

  • docs/adr/0001-rag-with-pgvector.md, with three decisions answered: why RAG and not fine-tuning, why pgvector and not a separate vector database, and one thing you chose not to build.
  • Every reason backed by evidence from your own runs, not by opinion.
  • The tempting alternative argued fairly, and a clear line about what you did not try.
Capstone: an ADR is what a reviewer reads to see how you think. It is also the best answer to the interview question “why did you build it this way?”.
What is real, and what is not. The numbers in the example ADR come from real runs of this project, listed below. The answers in the demo come from a stand-in writer, not a real model, and the ADR says so. Fine-tuning and a separate vector database were not tried, and the ADR says so too: an ADR that claims numbers it does not have is worse than none.

3 · Plan the work 10 min

Cut the work in small pieces:

  1. Collect evidence: run the scripts and write down every number and where it came from.
  2. Draft the context and the decision, in a few sentences.
  3. Write the reasons, each with its evidence.
  4. The alternatives, including the tempting one.
  5. The thing you did not build, and when to revisit.
  6. Read it as a stranger, cut, and commit.
# ADR NNNN: <the decision, as a short sentence>

Status: proposed | accepted | superseded by ADR NNNN
Date: YYYY-MM-DD

## Context          the situation and the forces: what is true, what is needed, what limits you
## Decision         what you will do, in a few sentences
## Why ...          the reasons, each with EVIDENCE (a number, a run, a test), not an opinion
## Alternatives considered   what else you could do, and why not (include the one that is tempting)
## One thing I chose not to build   and why, and when you would revisit
## Consequences     what gets easier, what gets harder, what is still unknown
A good ADR
  - is short (a page or two) and says WHY more than WHAT
  - gives evidence for every reason: a number, a run, a test, a link
  - includes the best alternative, argued fairly (even if it would win in some conditions)
  - says what is NOT known or NOT tried
  - says when to revisit the decision
  - is never rewritten: a new decision is a new ADR that 'supersedes' the old one

4 · Build 90 min

Step 1: collect the evidence

Gather the numbers from the earlier modules, and add the one that is new today: how long it takes to change one page of knowledge, and whether the next answer uses it. adr_evidence.py uses a scratch table, so your real data is not touched:

import os
import time
from dataclasses import replace
from pathlib import Path

from my_ai_project.access import allowed_kinds
from my_ai_project.chunking import chunk_documents
from my_ai_project.embedder import FastEmbedder
from my_ai_project.hybrid import KeywordIndex
from my_ai_project.knowledge_base import load_knowledge_base
from my_ai_project.rag import answer_question
from my_ai_project.simulated import SimulatedWriter
from my_ai_project.store import ChunkRecord, ChunkStore

DSN = os.environ.get("DATABASE_URL", "postgresql://assistant:assistant@localhost:5433/assistant")
store = ChunkStore(DSN, table="chunks_adr")   # a scratch table: the real one is not touched
store.drop()
store.init_schema()
embedder, writer = FastEmbedder(), SimulatedWriter()   # the writer is a stand-in, NOT a real model

documents = load_knowledge_base(Path("data/kb"))
by_id = {d.doc_id: d for d in documents}
records = [ChunkRecord(c.doc_id, c.index, by_id[c.doc_id].title, by_id[c.doc_id].kind, c.text, c.tokens) for c in chunk_documents(documents, max_tokens=40)]
store.add_chunks(records, embedder.embed([f"{r.title}. {r.text}" for r in records]))


def ask(question: str) -> str:
    keywords = KeywordIndex(store.all_chunks())   # rebuilt, because the keyword index is built once from the stored chunks
    result = answer_question(question, embedder, store, keywords, writer, allowed_kinds=allowed_kinds("customer"))
    return f"{result.text}   sources: {', '.join(s.citation for s in result.sources) or 'none'}"


QUESTION = "Which Wi-Fi band does the monitor support?"
print("before:", ask(QUESTION))

# The product changes: the monitor now supports 5 GHz too. Change ONE page, and nothing else.
page = next(r for r in records if r.doc_id == "faq-wifi")
changed = replace(page, text="The monitor supports 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks. Move it closer to the router and restart the monitor.")
start = time.perf_counter()
store.add_chunks([changed], embedder.embed([f"{changed.title}. {changed.text}"]))
elapsed = (time.perf_counter() - start) * 1000
print(f"update: re-embedded and stored 1 chunk in {elapsed:.0f} ms (no training, no new model)")
print("after: ", ask(QUESTION))
store.drop()

Step 2: write the ADR

This is the real ADR for my-ai-project. Read it as a model of the form. Do not copy it: your numbers, your context and your “one thing I did not build” are different, and an ADR with borrowed reasons is worthless:

# ADR 0001: Answer from the knowledge base with RAG on PostgreSQL and pgvector

Status: accepted
Date: 2027-01-30

## Context
The assistant answers questions about a fictional pump monitor from 20 pages (manuals, FAQs, error pages). The pages change.
Error pages are for staff only. Every answer must say where it came from. One person builds and runs it. Tests must run with
no model and no key.

## Decision
Chunk the pages, embed them with a small local model, store them in PostgreSQL with pgvector, retrieve with a vector search
plus a keyword search, and give the best chunks to the model as numbered sources. The answer cites them. The user's role
filters what can be retrieved. Fine-tuning is not used, and no separate vector database is run.

## Why RAG, and not fine-tuning
- Updating knowledge: changing one page took 13 ms (re-embed and store one chunk) and the next answer used it
  ("2.4 GHz networks only" became "2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks"). No training, no new model.
- Sources: every answer carries `[doc_id#chunk]`. A fine-tuned model cannot point to where a fact came from.
- Permissions: the role filter is in the query. Asked as a customer, 18 golden questions touched 0 of the 7 staff-only pages.
  Facts baked into a model's weights cannot be filtered per user.
- Measurement: retrieval and writing are scored apart (hit@3 100% while the whole pass rate is 61% with the stand-in writer),
  so I know which part to fix.
- Fine-tuning is good for style, format and special behaviour. That is not the problem here. I have not tried it and have
  not measured its cost, so I do not claim numbers for it.

## Why pgvector, and not a separate vector database
- PostgreSQL is already needed. One system to run, back up and secure. The role filter, the metadata and the vectors are in one query.
- Size: 26 chunks today; retrieval takes 6.5 ms (median), most of it embedding the question. In a test with 50,000 synthetic
  vectors, an exact search took 8.6 ms and an HNSW index 0.6 ms with 95% recall. There is room to grow.
- Revisit if the data reaches millions of vectors, if writes become heavy, or if a feature is missing.

## Alternatives considered
- Put all pages in every prompt. All 20 pages are 862 tokens. This works today and is simpler. Cost and noise grow with every
  page, and a prompt cannot hold a real knowledge base. I keep RAG for growth, roles and citations. If the pages stay this small,
  this alternative wins and RAG is the wrong choice.
- A fixed lookup for error codes (see `incident.py`). It is exact and instant (about 0.1 microseconds), and the vector search alone
  missed the page for "What does error E104 mean?". It is used as a tool, next to RAG.

## One thing I chose not to build
Multi-agent orchestration. One tool and one loop answer the questions, and the steps are predictable. The golden set is not
at its target yet (11 of 18 with the stand-in writer), so a more complex design would hide the real problems. Revisit if the
golden set contains tasks that one loop cannot do.

## Consequences
- Easy to update, cite and restrict. Easy to test.
- Retrieval quality is my job: the keyword search was needed for exact codes, and the golden set found a real miss (q14).
- Chunk size and the keyword index matter. The keyword index is built once and must be rebuilt after an update.
- Answer quality with a real model is not measured yet. All answer-level numbers here use a stand-in writer.

Step 3: read it as a stranger

Check it against the rules. Notice what the example does that many ADRs do not: it argues the alternative that would win in some conditions (put all pages in the prompt, 862 tokens), it says which things were not tried, and it gives a “revisit” condition for each choice.

Step 4: tell the agents

Add one line to AGENTS.md so that every session knows where decisions live:

- Decisions live in `docs/adr/`. Read them before changing the stack. A new decision is a new ADR; do not rewrite an old one.

5 · Stuck? anytime

  • You have no evidence for a reason: either run something to get it, or take the reason out. Say “not measured” where that is true.
  • It reads like an advertisement: you only argued for your choice. Add the best alternative and a case where it wins.
  • It is three pages: cut. A reader gives an ADR five minutes.
  • You are afraid the decision is wrong: that is what “revisit when” is for. A decision with a stated way out is stronger than one without.
  • Your “thing I did not build”: pick something that you were tempted by and could defend, such as a reranker, multi-agent orchestration, or a fine-tuned model. Say what would make you build it.
Questions a reviewer may ask about the example

“Your knowledge base is 862 tokens. Why not put it all in the prompt?” (The ADR answers: it works today and would win if the pages stay small; RAG is kept for growth, roles and citations.) “Why 13 ms and not training time?” (It measures a re-embed of one chunk, not model training: the comparison is in kind, and the ADR does not claim a fine-tuning number.) “Does it work with a real model?” (Not measured: the ADR says so.)

7 · Check yourself 5 min

  1. What are the parts of an ADR?
  2. Why include the alternative that would win in some conditions?
  3. Why is a “revisit when” line useful?
Answers
  1. Status and date, context, decision, the reasons with evidence, alternatives considered, one thing not built, and consequences.
  2. It shows that you weighed the options honestly, and it tells the reader when your decision stops being right.
  3. A decision is right for some conditions. Naming them gives a clear signal to change course, and makes the decision safe to take.
