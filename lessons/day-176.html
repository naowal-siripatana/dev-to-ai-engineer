DAY 176 · FINAL WORKSHOP · DAY 2

Final workshop, Day 2, and the six-month retrospective

8 hhands-onfree
Today you will be able to give a second demo, look back on six months with evidence, and plan the next three.

1 · Before you start 10 min

Be clear about what this page is. The workshop is a live event: this page is a guide and a set of templates. The numbers in the example retrospective are real, from the course capstone as checked on one laptop; yours must come from your runs. The Docker images, the GitHub pipeline and the hosted model were never run in that checking, and the example says so.

Today’s goal: a second demo that is better than yesterday’s, honest feedback, and a look back at six months that ends with a plan for the next three. Same rule as yesterday: no new features.

2 · The plan for the day 10 min

Start with the system and the checks, then the fixes, then the demo, then the part that is only for you.

3 · Fixes, practice and the final demo round 180 min

Run your preflight and your demo check first. Check the three fixes from yesterday, and look at your rehearsal sheet: what did you change? Run the demo once in full. Then the final round, in a different order from yesterday, with the same sheets. Notice what changed between your two demos: that difference is what the last four weeks bought you.

An example of how the evidence can look (real numbers from the course capstone; write yours):

You should see
EXAMPLE (the numbers are real, from the course capstone on one laptop; write yours from your own runs)

Then the questions, with an example for each answer:

LOOKING BACK (2 to 3 lines each, with an example)
1. What did I learn that I could not have learned from reading? (a thing that broke)
2. Which habit helped most? (the fresh clone, the test that fails first, saying what is a stand-in, the weekly plan)
3. Which habit do I still lack? (and the one tiny experiment that starts it)
4. What did I believe six months ago that I no longer believe?
5. What am I proud of, that I can SHOW?
6. What did I never run, and does it still matter to me?
7. Who helped, and have I said thank you?

5 · What next: three months 45 min

One page, written today, while the six months are fresh. Make it small enough to do: a goal you can check, a weekly rhythm, one improvement a month (start with the list of what you never ran), and a first Sunday of the month when you look at the log and change one thing.

WHAT NEXT: THREE MONTHS (one page)
the goal (one sentence, a result I can check):          ...
every week:   3 to 5 applications (45 minutes each, in the log)    + one person I write to about their work
every month:  one improvement that makes the project more real:    first from the list "what I never ran"
the project:  a 10-user trial / a real login / a bigger set of questions   (pick ONE)
learning:     one topic, one small thing built, one note explaining it
people:       who I ask for feedback, and when
how I check: the first Sunday of each month: the log, the project, the list; change ONE thing

If you want to share the journey, here is a shape for a short public post. Only things you can show; say what is a stand-in and what you never ran.

OPTIONAL: A PUBLIC POST (about 150 words; only things you can show, and say what is a stand-in)
I spent six months learning to build AI systems, and wrote down everything I ran and everything I did not.
The project: [one line]. What runs: [the parts]. What is a stand-in: [the writer, ...]. What I never ran: [the list].
Three things I learned: [a bug that taught me something; a decision with its evidence; a habit].
The code and a 3-minute demo: [links]. I am looking for: [a role / feedback / a team].

6 · Check yourself 5 min

  1. What is the evidence for each skill on your list?
  2. What is on your list of what you never ran, and which one will you run first?
  3. What is the one thing you will change after the first month?
Notes

There is no right answer here. If you cannot answer one of them, go back to the section that makes you write it down: it is the whole point of today.
